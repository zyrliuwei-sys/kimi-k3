## Compare the workflow, not a single sample

One attractive image is not enough to choose an AI image generator. The useful question is whether the tool helps you make a repeatable result for the work you actually have: a campaign, product study, presentation, social post, or visual exploration.

Run the same brief through each option. Give every tool the same subject, desired style, aspect ratio, and constraint. Then compare how many rounds it takes before you would be comfortable sharing the result.

## The controls that matter most

Look for these practical controls:

1. **Frame selection** — can you choose a useful aspect ratio before generating?
2. **Reference support** — can you add a visual source when words are insufficient?
3. **Variation workflow** — can you keep a good direction and adjust one decision at a time?
4. **Result access** — can you review, revisit, and download a generation without losing the prompt?
5. **Clear usage information** — are the available credits, plan rules, and applicable terms easy to find?

An image tool does not need every advanced setting to be useful. For many teams, a quick prompt, a frame selector, a reference upload, and a clear history are the controls that keep experimentation from turning into repeated guesswork.

## Treat price as a current product detail

Free access can mean introductory credits, a limited trial, local software, or a no-cost account tier. Plans and model availability change frequently, so do not make a decision from an old comparison table. Check the current pricing and terms directly before a purchase, then estimate your own cost from the number of useful images you expect to make—not only the first image.

## Keep a human final check

AI generation accelerates exploration, but it does not remove responsibility for final assets. Review claims, typography, brand use, people, and the rights to any reference you supplied. A good workflow makes that review easy by keeping the result and its prompt close together.
